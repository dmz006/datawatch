Usage calculator, index of the three variants

Usage calculator mockups

On every usage reading the calculator tells the operator the weekly percent, the days since the weekly reset, the pace line, the burn per 24 hours (both the recent burn and the week average), the projected end at the reset and the time the allowance would run out, and whether the burn is within budget or over budget. The operator chooses the number of Claude lanes; the calculator only advises.

The three usage policies

The operator picks one usage policy. Every variant page has a policy selector with these three choices, and the policy changes only the advice, never the numbers of the reading.

  • Run until used up: spend the whole weekly allowance, because unused points are lost at the reset. The advice is the largest lane count that projects to 100 percent or less at the reset.
  • Pace to the week: last until the reset with a margin of 5 points, so the week does not run out before its end. The advice is the largest lane count that projects to 95 percent or less.
  • Run flat out: finish the remaining work before the allowance does. All lanes run, and the calculator checks the work against the allowance without slowing any lane.

The three variants

Each variant reads the same reading and calls the same model, so the figures agree. The variants differ in layout and in which field comes first.

Pace gauge

It shows the verdict first, then the weekly percent on a gauge with the pace line and the two projections to the reset. The reading fields and a chart of the readings since the reset follow it.

Data it needs: the weekly percent and the time it was read, the weekly reset day and time, and the previous reading from the operator's log for the recent burn. The session percent is optional.

Lane planner

It shows the verdict first, then a table of what each lane count projects to at the reset, with the advice and the chosen count marked. A timeline from the reading to the reset follows the table.

Data it needs: the weekly percent and the time it was read, the weekly reset, the previous reading, the number of lanes that ran in the recent window, the idle baseline (3 points per 24 hours by default), the safety margin and the remaining work for Run flat out.

Open the lane planner mockup

Burn ledger

It shows the verdict and the reading fields first, then the points of weekly allowance used on each day since the reset. A cost section follows, with the planning coefficients and the points per 24 hours at each lane count.

Data it needs: the weekly percent and the time it was read, the previous reading from the operator's log, and the readings since the reset. The split of each day by kind of work is an illustration until attribution data exists.

  • Pace line 79.8 percent, so the reading is 7.8 points under pace. Hand arithmetic on the rounded 5.59 days gives 79.9; the model works from the timestamps and shows 79.8.

About these pages

These pages are mockups built on real operator-reported readings. Nothing is saved or sent from them. The commands in the text forms are proposals, and the final names are not decided. The choice of variant is the operator's.
